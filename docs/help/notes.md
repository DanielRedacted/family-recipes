Notes are for things worth remembering about a recipe: "Our oven runs hot, check at 40 minutes", "Bread flour works better here", "Doubled this for Thanksgiving". Each note is tied to the words it's about, which are highlighted in the recipe.

## Leave a note

1. Open the recipe. You don't need to edit it.
2. Select the words the note is about, such as an ingredient or a step.
3. Tap **Add note** under the words.
4. Type the note and tap **Save**.

To leave a note about the whole recipe instead, tap **Add note** at the top of the **Notes** list.

A note can link to other recipes, people and ingredients the same way as the whiteboard: **[[Pie Crust]]**, **@Grandma**, **#butter**.

## See the notes

Notes show under the recipe, or beside it on a wide screen. Each shows the words it's about, who wrote it and when. The name opens that person's page, which lists every note they've left. A note is credited only to the person who wrote it: it never makes them one of the recipe's authors or puts them in its history. Tap the highlighted words to find their note, or tap the words at the top of a note to find them in the recipe.

Tap **Hide** to take the notes and highlights away while you cook, and **Show** to bring them back. The choice is remembered on this device for every recipe.

If you make more or less of the recipe, switch to metric, or pick another version, the words of a note can change. Those notes stay in the list with **Not found in the recipe as shown**, and come back to their place when you change it back.

## Change or delete a note

Anyone who can edit recipes can change or delete any note, using the pencil and the bin under it. A changed note shows who changed it.

## Who can write notes

Everyone sees the notes. Writing, changing and deleting them needs editing rights: the Mac, or a phone or tablet signed in with the PIN (see [Getting started](#/settings/help/getting-started)).

Notes are kept in the recipe itself, so they go wherever the recipe goes, such as to GitHub or another copy of the recipes folder. They never change the recipe's own text.
